About

Biogeochemist studying watershed-estuary nutrient dynamics.

I am an aquatic biogeochemist working at the intersection of watershed science, sediment chemistry, and coastal water quality. My research focuses on how phosphorus and carbon move through river networks, sediments, and estuarine systems, and how those processes influence harmful algal blooms and ecosystem health. Much of my work centers on the Lake Pontchartrain Basin in coastal Louisiana, where I combine field sampling, sediment core incubations, laboratory geochemistry, geospatial analysis, and long-term environmental datasets to understand both external nutrient loading and internal sediment nutrient release.

Research

Current themes

Legacy phosphorus in river sediments

Examining how redox conditions, dredging, and sediment age influence phosphorus release from subtropical tributaries flowing into the Lake Pontchartrain estuary.

Shear stress and internal loading

Evaluating how wind-driven sediment disturbance can mobilize bioavailable phosphorus and contribute to eutrophic conditions and harmful algal blooms in shallow estuarine systems.

Watershed-to-estuary nutrient pathways

Linking watershed loading, sediment storage, and coastal response using field data, long-term records, and geospatial analysis to support water quality management.

Methods

How I work

  • Watershed-scale nutrient and discharge analysis
  • Intact sediment-core incubations and flux experiments
  • Redox, dredging, and shear-stress process studies
  • Geospatial analysis, kriging, and mapping
  • Long-term water-quality synthesis for management questions

Biogeochemistry

A clearer phosphorus biogeochemistry diagram

This diagram simplifies the system into a left-to-right pathway: watershed inputs, river transport, estuarine water-column processing, sediment storage and release, and ecological response. The layout is meant to make the sediment-water exchange step visually clear instead of burying it in too many arrows.

1. Watershed inputs
Phosphorus enters from runoff, erosion, and upstream sources.
2. Transport
Rivers move dissolved and particulate phosphorus downstream.
3. Sediment exchange
Particles settle to sediments, then phosphorus can be released back under anoxia or disturbance.
4. Ecological response
That balance helps determine bloom risk, retention, and water-quality outcomes.
